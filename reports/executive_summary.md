# Executive summary

## Business question

If an international company had to prioritize three Latin American markets for commercial expansion, which countries should enter the pipeline first based on public, comparable evidence?

## Result

Using the latest complete common year (2024), the model ranks **Mexico, Brazil, and Chile** as the three priority markets among the ten countries assessed.

- **Mexico (69.9)** offers the strongest balance between market attractiveness and commercial accessibility. It is the most robust top-three choice in the sensitivity test, remaining there in 99.7% of 2,000 weight scenarios.
- **Brazil (67.6)** has the highest market-attractiveness score, driven by scale, but its accessibility profile is less favorable. It remains in the top three in 91.1% of simulations.
- **Chile (60.7)** offsets its smaller scale with the highest commercial-accessibility score. It remains in the top three in 76.9% of simulations.
- **Costa Rica (59.6)** is the principal challenger. Its 32.3% top-three probability shows that modest changes in strategic priorities can move it ahead of Chile.

## Commercial interpretation

The ranking should guide the first stage of market screening, not replace due diligence. A practical portfolio strategy would be:

1. Treat Mexico as the balanced anchor market.
2. Pursue Brazil when addressable scale justifies greater operating complexity.
3. Compare Chile and Costa Rica at the sector level before committing resources: Chile leads in the base case, but this decision is sensitive to how the company values scale versus access.

## Method and limitations

Eight World Bank indicators were winsorized, normalized to 0–100, and combined using documented weights. A 2,000-run weight sensitivity analysis tests ranking stability. The model does not include sector demand, competition, regulation, taxes, security, route-to-market costs, or product-market fit. Those factors belong in the next due-diligence stage.
